Forty-one dead sessions found pinning a working Mac

The more agents you run, the less machine you keep. A census, not a product launch.

By midweek the machine is sand. Every agentic session — Claude, Codex, Cursor — hires a private staff of tool servers. The editor closes. The staff remains. launchd, that old butler, adopts them. Activity Monitor, asked who is eating the house, answers only node.

“macOS does not kill politely. It kills the thread you were working in.”

On one 16 GB M4 the first pass counted 41 orphaned trees, about four gigabytes of swap, and a pressure drop from 91 percent to 55 after the reap. No live session was touched. Tests: nine of nine.

There are two remedies. One is a 64 GB laptop. The other is a script. The dry-run is read-only and lasts five seconds. Continued on page 2, the handwritten account.

In brief

Kill-list of two classes only: fleets parented by launchd older than two hours; sessions with zero CPU for six. All else is advice.
